      Harry was staring at him, alarmed; Filch had never looked madder. His eyes were popping, a tic 
was going in one of his pouchy cheeks, and the tartan scarf didn’t help. 
 
      “Very well — go — and don’t breathe a word — not that — however, if you didn’t read — go 
now, I have to write up Peeves’ report — go —” 
 
      Amazed at his luck, Harry sped out of the office, up the corridor, and back upstairs. To escape 
from Filch’s office without punishment was probably some kind of school record. 
 
      “Harry! Harry! Did it work?” 
 
      Nearly Headless Nick came gliding out of a classroom. Behind him, Harry could see the 
wreckage of a large black-and-gold cabinet that appeared to have been dropped from a great 
height. 
 
      “I persuaded Peeves to crash it right over Filch’s office,” said Nick eagerly. “Thought it might 
distract him —” 
 
      “Was that you?” said Harry gratefully. “Yeah, it worked, I didn’t even get detention. Thanks, 
Nick!” 
 
      They set off up the corridor together. Nearly Headless Nick, Harry noticed, was still holding Sir 
Patrick’s rejection letter…  
 
      “I wish there was something I could do for you about the Headless Hunt,” Harry said. Nearly 
Headless Nick stopped in his tracks and Harry walked right through him. He wished he hadn’t; it 
was like stepping through an icy shower. 
 
      “But there is something you could do for me,” said Nick excitedly. “Harry — would I be asking 
too much — but no, you wouldn’t want —” 
 
      “What is it?” said Harry.

himself up and looking dignified.
